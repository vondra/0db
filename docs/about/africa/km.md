## Road traffic

### Class defaults only

The Direction Générale des Routes publishes no open AADT. CNOSSOS class defaults applied with Moroni Tier-1 boost (×2.0). Three islands — Grande Comore (Ngazidja), Mohéli (Mwali), Anjouan (Nzwani) — each have separate road networks with no fixed links between them.

## Railway

### Comoros has no railway.

No railway exists or has been planned. All inter-island transport is by ferry or light aircraft.

## Industrial

### GEM — 1 plant, ~3 MW

- **Voidjou** (Grande Comore, ~3 MW, diesel) — only GEM-listed plant; MA-MWE-operated, main Moroni-grid supply

The national utility **MA-MWE** (Mamwe — Eau et Électricité) operates diesel thermal generators on all three islands. Chronic power cuts of 4–20 hours/day are common. A newer 6.3 MW solar farm at Moroni (ADFD-funded) and most diesel plants fall below GEM thresholds.

## Notes

The Comoros produce ~80% of the world's ylang-ylang, a key perfume ingredient (Chanel No. 5). Vanilla and cloves are also significant exports. Mount Karthala (2,361 m) on Grande Comore is an active stratovolcano with one of the world's largest calderas; it erupted in 2005 and 2006. Comoros has experienced more than 20 coups or coup attempts since independence in 1975. The fourth island, Mayotte, voted to remain French in a 2009 referendum and became a French overseas département in 2011 — the Comorian government continues to claim it. Prince Said Ibrahim International Airport (HAH) near Moroni handles international connections via Air Austral and Ethiopian Airlines.
