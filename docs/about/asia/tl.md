## Road traffic

### Class defaults only

The Ministry of Public Works publishes no open AADT. CNOSSOS class defaults applied with Dili Tier-1 boost (×2.0) and Baucau Tier-2 boost (×1.4). Road network OSM coverage is approximately 50% — many rural roads and mountain tracks are unmapped.

**154k road segments enriched (50% coverage).** The AH25 Asian Highway (Dili ↔ Baucau ↔ Los Palos) and the southern coastal route are the main freight corridors.

## Railway

### Timor-Leste has no railway.

No railway was ever constructed. The rugged mountainous terrain (Ramelau 2,963 m) makes construction extremely costly.

## Industrial

### GEM — 2 plants, ~255 MW

- **Hera Power Station** (~30 MW diesel thermal) — east of Dili; primary grid supply
- **Metinaro** — smaller diesel plant

The offshore **Bayu-Undan gas/condensate field** (JPDA, now largely depleted) was the primary revenue source since 2004. Greater Sunrise (estimated 5.1 TCF gas) remains contested with Australia under the 2018 maritime boundary treaty — not yet developed.

## Notes

Timor-Leste restored independence on 20 May 2002 — the newest country in Asia, and one of the world's newest overall, following the 1999 referendum and UN transitional administration (UNTAET). The country occupies the eastern half of Timor island; the western half is Indonesian West Timor (Nusa Tenggara Timur). Portuguese and Tetum are official languages. Nicolau Lobato International Airport (DIL) in Dili handles all international flights. Road noise is dominated by Dili urban traffic and the Dili–Baucau national highway.
